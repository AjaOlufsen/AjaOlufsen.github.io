Viking Ship Museum App

Interaction Design · Digital Museum Experience · Prototyping · 2024

A playful digital museum experience combining QR-code interaction, exploration and historical storytelling through an interactive treasure hunt.

Interaction Design Design Sprint 2.0 p5.js User Testing
About

Exploring how digital interaction could make a museum visit more active, playful and engaging.

The project was developed around a case from the Viking Ship Museum in Roskilde, which wanted to explore new ways of communicating activities, information and historical content through digital technology.

We focused on how a mobile experience could motivate visitors to move through the museum and actively discover content rather than receive information passively.

The challenge

Making museum information easier to discover and engage with.

Existing information displays could be difficult for international visitors to interpret, while some areas of the museum required alternative ways of communicating historical knowledge when staff were not present.

The concept

Turning the museum visit into an interactive treasure hunt.

The concept evolved into a mobile experience aimed primarily at younger visitors.

Guests could scan QR codes positioned throughout the museum, meet animated Viking characters and complete missions to unlock the next part of the experience.

How can learning feel like exploration rather than instruction?
Design process

Moving from speculative ideas towards a testable museum experience.

The project developed through several iterations, beginning with speculative design and the question: What if Vikings still existed in contemporary society?

This exploration gradually moved towards a more concrete experience. We used ideation, storyboarding and Design Sprint 2.0 to develop the interaction and explore different combinations of navigation, storytelling and play.

A functional prototype was developed with a particular focus on QR-code interaction as the connection between the physical museum and the digital experience.

User testing

Testing whether play and learning could work as one experience.

The prototype was tested with users and museum staff to evaluate both the interface and the overall visitor experience.

Participants responded positively to the interactive treasure hunt, particularly the animated Viking characters and the use of QR codes to progress through the experience.

The testing helped refine the balance between entertainment and historical learning, so information felt integrated into the activity rather than added afterwards.

Design approach

Connecting the digital interface to movement through the physical museum.

The app was not designed as a standalone screen experience. The QR-code interaction made the museum itself part of the interface, requiring visitors to move, discover and interact with physical locations.

This relationship between physical exploration and digital feedback became central to the concept.
