Barnabókaklubbin

3rd Semester Project · Digital Transformation · RPA · 2025

A digital transformation case exploring how robotic process automation can support membership onboarding in a small organisation while preserving the human judgement and local knowledge embedded in everyday work.

RPA Power Automate Desktop Process Mapping Service Blueprinting Qualitative Research

Context

Barnabókaklubbin is a Faroese children's book club whose everyday administration relies on several loosely connected systems.

Membership administration takes place across tools such as Outlook, Excel, Navision 2018 and Brevo, requiring staff to manually move information between systems and rely on tacit knowledge to keep processes consistent.

Research focus

The project investigated how Robotic Process Automation could support membership onboarding in a small organisation without removing the human judgement needed in exceptions and less structured situations.

Rather than treating automation as a purely technical task, we examined the relationship between workflows, organisational knowledge and opportunities for process improvement.

The challenge

Because the systems were not fully integrated, repetitive administrative work depended heavily on manual coordination.

This created both inefficiencies and organisational vulnerability: critical knowledge about members, payments and shipments could become difficult to access when the person holding the overview was unavailable.

Our approach

We first mapped the existing workflow in detail to understand how information moved between systems and where manual work occurred.

This allowed us to distinguish between repetitive tasks suitable for automation and activities that still depended on human judgement.

Automation was not the starting point. Understanding the process was.

The solution

Based on the process analysis, we redesigned parts of the membership onboarding workflow and developed an RPA prototype using Microsoft Power Automate Desktop.

The automation reduced repetitive manual handling while retaining points where staff could review, interpret or intervene when needed.

My role

I worked across process analysis, workflow design and technical development of the automation solution.

My contribution focused on translating the existing administrative workflow into a structured digital process and helping identify which parts could be automated reliably.

Process optimisation

The aim was not to automate the existing workflow exactly as it was. Mapping the process revealed opportunities to simplify and restructure tasks before introducing automation.

This helped avoid reproducing inefficient manual practices inside a new digital workflow.

Digital transformation perspective

The case demonstrated how relatively small automation interventions can act as pragmatic steps in organisations where replacing an entire system landscape is unrealistic.

The project therefore approached RPA not only as a productivity tool, but as part of a broader organisational transformation process.
