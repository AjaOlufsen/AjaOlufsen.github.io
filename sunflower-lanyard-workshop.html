Sunflower Lanyard Workshop

Participatory Workshop · Roskilde Festival · 2025

A co-creative workshop inviting participants to personalise the Sunflower Lanyard and explore identity, visibility and ownership through hands-on making.

Co-creation Workshop Facilitation Material Exploration Participatory Design

Context

The workshop was developed as a participatory extension of our broader work with the Sunflower Lanyard and invisible disabilities.

At Roskilde Festival, participants were invited to work directly with the lanyard and reinterpret its visual identity through making.

Workshop focus

Rather than asking participants to discuss identity and visibility only through conversation, the workshop used material making as the entry point.

This created a space where personal expression, interpretation and conversation could emerge through the activity itself.

Making as conversation

Rather than presenting participants with a fixed message, the workshop used hands-on making as a way to open conversations.

Participants could personalise their lanyards and explore how visibility, recognition and identity might be expressed differently.

Facilitation

The workshop was designed as an open and informal environment where participants could engage at their own pace.

Our role was to introduce the activity, support the making process and facilitate conversations without prescribing what the final lanyard should represent.

Material exploration

Participants were given access to a range of tactile and decorative materials that could be combined with the original lanyard.

The material palette was intentionally open-ended, allowing people to experiment rather than follow a predefined visual outcome.

Co-creation

Instead of designing a new symbol on behalf of users, the workshop shifted authorship towards the people engaging with the lanyard.

Each redesigned lanyard therefore became a personal interpretation rather than a standardised design solution.

Experience

The workshop explored what happens when participants are given greater ownership over a symbol usually defined by a fixed and recognisable visual identity.

Outcome

Through making and conversation, participants transformed a standardised symbol into individual expressions of visibility, identity and personal meaning.
