Sunflower Lanyard Workshop

Participatory Workshop · Roskilde Festival · 2025

A co-creative workshop inviting participants to personalise the Sunflower Lanyard and explore identity, visibility and ownership through hands-on making.

Co-creation Workshop Facilitation Material Exploration Participatory Design
About

Creating space for participants to reshape the meaning of a familiar symbol.

At Roskilde Festival, participants were invited to engage creatively with the Sunflower Lanyard through a hands-on making workshop.

Using the lanyard as both material and starting point, the workshop created space for conversation, reflection and personal expression around identity, visibility and invisible disabilities.

Making as conversation

Using hands-on making to open conversations naturally.

Rather than presenting participants with a fixed message, the workshop used making as a way to explore personal interpretations of the lanyard.

Participants could personalise their lanyards and experiment with how visibility, recognition and identity might be expressed differently.

The act of making became a social interaction in itself, allowing conversations to emerge informally around the table.

Facilitation

The workshop was designed as an open and informal environment where participants could engage at their own pace.

Our role was to introduce the activity, support the making process and facilitate conversations without prescribing what the final lanyard should represent.

Co-creation

Rather than designing a new symbol on behalf of users, the workshop shifted authorship towards the people engaging with the lanyard.

This allowed individual expression and lived experience to become part of the design process itself.

Materials

Treating material choice as a tool for self-expression.

Participants were given access to a range of tactile and decorative materials that could be combined with the original lanyard.

The open material palette encouraged experimentation rather than guiding participants towards a single visual outcome.

This made each redesigned lanyard a personal interpretation rather than a standardised solution.

Experience

From standardised symbol to personal expression.

The workshop explored what happens when participants are given greater ownership over a symbol normally defined by a fixed and recognisable visual identity.

Through making, conversation and co-creation, the activity invited participants to consider how visibility and identity can be negotiated rather than simply communicated.
