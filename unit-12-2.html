UNIT 12:2

Interactive Installation · Steno Museum · 2026

About

UNIT 12:2 — The Future Consultation is an interactive museum installation developed in collaboration with Steno Museum in Aarhus. The project explores contraception, responsibility and gender through a speculative consultation with a robotic doctor.

Designed for young museum visitors, the installation asks participants to make decisions about protection, duration and responsibility before receiving a personalised contraceptive recommendation.

The concept

Rather than simply presenting information about contraception, the installation deliberately challenges expectations about who is responsible for preventing pregnancy.

After answering a series of questions, visitors can receive an unexpected or mismatched contraceptive method. A boy might, for example, be prescribed the pill. The mismatch is used as a design intervention to make assumptions about gender, responsibility and reproductive labour visible.

The interaction

Visitors enter a fictional future consultation and interact with the robotic doctor through a sequence of questions. The system responds through movement, sound and a physical printed receipt.

The receipt presents the suggested contraceptive method together with information about use, protection and side effects. It also introduces facts about the unequal number of contraceptive options available to men and women, encouraging visitors to compare their recommendation with existing reproductive realities.

My role

I developed the project as part of a three-person design team. My work included concept development, interaction design, Research through Design, user research, prototyping and the physical and technical development of the installation.

The process included workshops with young people, iterative prototype testing and continuous development of both the interaction and the physical expression of the robotic consultation.

Design approach

The project used speculative and discursive design to create reflection rather than provide a single correct answer. Instead of designing a neutral information interface, we used discomfort, surprise and role reversal as active parts of the interaction.

Through workshops and user testing, we investigated how teenagers understood different contraceptive methods and how they discussed responsibility when confronted with scenarios that challenged familiar gender roles.
