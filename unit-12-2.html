UNIT 12:2 — The Future Consultation

Interactive Installation · Steno Museum · 2026

A speculative robotic consultation exploring contraception, gender and reproductive responsibility through an intentionally unsettling museum experience.

Research through Design Speculative Design Physical Computing Interaction Design
About

A fictional future consultation designed to challenge familiar ideas about contraception.

UNIT 12:2 — The Future Consultation was developed in collaboration with Steno Museum in Aarhus.

The installation invites young museum visitors into a speculative consultation with a robotic doctor, where decisions about protection, duration and responsibility lead to a personalised contraceptive recommendation.

The concept

Using mismatch to make invisible assumptions visible.

Rather than simply presenting information, the installation deliberately disrupts expectations about who should be responsible for preventing pregnancy.

A participant may receive a contraceptive method that does not traditionally correspond to their gender — for example, a boy may be prescribed the pill.

This mismatch functions as a design intervention, creating space for reflection on gender roles, reproductive labour and unequal contraceptive responsibility.

My role

I developed the project as part of a three-person design team. My work included concept development, interaction design, Research through Design, user research and prototyping.

I also contributed to the physical and technical development of the installation.

Process

The process included workshops with young people, iterative prototype testing and continuous development of both the interaction and the physical expression of the robotic consultation.

Insights from testing were used to refine both the scenarios and the balance between information, surprise and discomfort.

The interaction

From consultation to printed recommendation.

Visitors interact with the robotic doctor through a sequence of questions about protection, duration and responsibility.

The robot responds through movement and sound before producing a physical printed receipt containing the suggested contraceptive method.

The receipt also presents information about use, side effects, protection and the unequal number of contraceptive options available to men and women.

Design approach

Designing for reflection rather than giving the visitor a correct answer.

The project draws on speculative and discursive design to use discomfort, surprise and role reversal as active parts of the museum experience.

Through workshops and testing, we explored how teenagers understood contraception and how they negotiated responsibility when familiar gender roles were challenged.
