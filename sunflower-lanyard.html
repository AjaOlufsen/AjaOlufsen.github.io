The Sunflower Lanyard - Staring Right Back

2nd Semester Project · Critical Design · Interactive Installation · 2025

A critical design project exploring how the Sunflower Lanyard shapes social perception of invisible disabilities — and how interactive design can turn the experience of being observed back towards the observer.

Critical Design Research through Design Embodied Interaction HCI Qualitative Research

Context

The Sunflower Lanyard is designed to signal that a person may have an invisible disability or condition and may need additional understanding, time or support.

Our project explored the tension within that visibility: a symbol intended to create understanding can also change how a person is perceived, categorised or looked at in public space.

Research focus

Rather than redesigning the lanyard as a product, we investigated the social dynamics surrounding it through Critical Design and Research through Design.

We were particularly interested in how stigma, visibility and the social gaze could be made tangible through interaction.

Staring Right Back

Staring Right Back was developed as an immersive installation exploring the bodily experience of being watched, judged and socially exposed.

Projected eyes and recorded voices turn the gaze towards the participant, shifting their position from observer to observed.

Embodied Mirroring

Through the project we developed the concept of Embodied Mirroring: a design approach in which the participant is placed in a situation that reflects aspects of another person's embodied experience.

Instead of only explaining stigma conceptually, the installation uses the participant's own body as part of the reflection.

What changes when the person looking suddenly becomes the person being looked at?

Research & process

The project developed through exploratory experiments, prototyping and iterative testing.

Early investigations examined how the Sunflower Lanyard influenced first impressions and social interpretation.

These findings gradually shifted the project from observation towards more embodied and affective design experiments.

Design approach

The project used discomfort, visibility and role reversal as design material rather than attempting to solve stigma through a conventional product intervention.

Prototypes were treated as questions that could make otherwise invisible social dynamics physically and emotionally present.

The installation

Projection, interactive visuals and audio were combined to create an immersive environment in which the participant became the focus of attention.

The physical setup intensified the sensation of exposure and made the relationship between looking and being looked at difficult to ignore.

Outcome

The final installation translated an abstract social phenomenon into a bodily interaction.

Rather than communicating a fixed message, the experience was designed to create reflection through the participant's own encounter with visibility and judgement.
