The Contraception Line

Interactive Installation · Roskilde Festival · 2026

An interactive telephone installation exploring contraception, responsibility and sexual health through anonymous participation at Roskilde Festival.

Interaction Design Physical Computing Arduino Prototyping

Context

The Contraception Line was developed for Roskilde Festival as a playful and anonymous way of opening conversations about contraception, sexual health and responsibility.

Rather than presenting festival visitors with static information, the installation invited them to actively participate and contribute anonymous responses to a shared picture of behaviour and responsibility.

The interaction

Visitors interact with the installation through a telephone receiver and physical buttons.

A short series of questions asks about sexual activity, contraception and who took responsibility for protection. The interaction was deliberately kept simple so it could work naturally within a busy festival environment.

My role

I worked on the concept, interaction design, physical prototyping and technical development of the installation.

This included developing the interaction flow and translating the concept into a physical experience that could operate reliably during repeated festival use.

Process

The project developed through iterative cycles of concept development, electronics prototyping, programming and physical testing.

A key focus was balancing technical robustness with an interaction that remained immediately understandable to festival visitors.

Design approach

The familiar telephone receiver acted as an intuitive invitation to interact, reducing the need for lengthy instructions.

Physical buttons kept the interaction direct and accessible, while the anonymous format allowed visitors to engage with a sensitive topic without publicly disclosing personal information.

Experience

The installation used participation rather than passive information to make contraception and responsibility part of the festival experience.

By combining an everyday object with a simple interactive flow, the project created a low-threshold entry point into a topic that can otherwise feel private or difficult to discuss.
