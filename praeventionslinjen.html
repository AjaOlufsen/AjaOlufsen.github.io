The Contraception Line

Interactive Installation · Roskilde Festival · 2026

An interactive telephone installation exploring contraception, responsibility and sexual health through anonymous participation at Roskilde Festival.

Interaction Design Physical Computing Arduino Prototyping

Context

The Contraception Line was developed for Roskilde Festival as a playful and anonymous way of opening conversations about contraception, sexual health and responsibility.

Rather than presenting visitors with static information, the installation invited them to participate and contribute their own experiences to a shared representation of festival behaviour.

Project snapshot

Year: 2026

Context: Roskilde Festival

Type: Interactive installation

Interaction: Telephone + physical buttons

Role: Concept, interaction design, prototyping & technical development

The interaction

Turning an everyday object into an anonymous conversation.

Visitors interact with the installation through a telephone receiver and physical buttons.

A short series of questions asks about sexual activity, contraception and responsibility. Responses are collected anonymously, allowing the installation to build a picture of how protection is approached among festival visitors.

My role

I worked across the concept, interaction design, physical prototyping and technical development of the installation.

This included developing the interaction flow and translating the concept into a robust physical experience suitable for a festival environment.

Process

The project moved through iterative cycles of concept development, physical prototyping, programming and testing.

Particular attention was given to keeping the interaction simple and intuitive while ensuring that the electronics could function reliably during repeated use.

Design approach

Designing for participation in a busy festival environment.

The interaction needed to be understandable without lengthy instructions and quick enough to fit naturally into the rhythm of a festival.

The familiar telephone receiver created a clear invitation to interact, while the simple physical controls kept the experience accessible and reduced the need for screen-based navigation.
