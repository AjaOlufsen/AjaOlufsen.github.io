Contraception & Responsibility Workshop

Master’s Thesis Research · Participatory Design · School Workshops · 2026

A facilitated design research workshop exploring young people's knowledge of contraception and how they negotiate gender, responsibility and reproductive choices.

Participatory Design Workshop Facilitation User Research Qualitative Research

Context

The workshop was developed as part of the research process for our Master's thesis and the Future Consultation installation at Steno Museum.

Working directly with young people allowed us to investigate how contraception was understood before translating those insights into the final interactive installation.

Research focus

We explored what participants already knew about different contraceptive methods, how responsibility was negotiated between genders and how young people responded when familiar roles and expectations were challenged.

The workshop was designed to generate discussion rather than lead participants towards a predetermined correct answer.

The workshop

We designed a series of participatory activities using visual mapping exercises, contraceptive method cards and discussion-based scenarios.

The activities invited participants to share knowledge, assumptions and opinions while allowing us to observe how different perspectives were negotiated within the group.

Facilitation

We facilitated the sessions ourselves, introducing activities, guiding conversations and observing group dynamics.

Because contraception can be a sensitive topic, participation was structured so students could contribute without being asked to disclose personal experiences.

Research insights

Condoms and birth control pills were widely recognised, while methods such as the vaginal ring, contraceptive patch, diaphragm and female condom were much less familiar.

Discussions also revealed differences in how contraceptive responsibility was understood and negotiated.

From research to design

Findings from the workshops were incorporated directly into the design of the Future Consultation.

Participant responses helped us refine the scenarios, identify where additional information was needed and understand which assumptions about gender and responsibility the final installation could meaningfully challenge.

Key observations

Familiarity with contraceptive methods was uneven.

Gender influenced how responsibility was discussed.

Humour and uncertainty often became part of the conversation.
